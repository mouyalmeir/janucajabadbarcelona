How to light the menorah

A practical, step-by-step guide so that every home in Barcelona shines over these eight nights.

No menorah? Order your kit

Men and women alike are obligated to light the menorah or to take part in the family lighting. Children are also encouraged to light their own menorahs, and students or people who live alone should light it in their own home or room.

What do you need?

  1. A menorah. It must have eight holders in a row at the same height and a ninth, separate holder (higher or set apart) for the shamash: the helper candle used to light all the others.
  2. 44 candles (or olive oil and cotton wicks). Since the miracle happened with olive oil, an oil menorah is ideal, although beeswax or paraffin candles are perfectly fine (electric or gas lights may not be used). You will need 36 candles for the eight nights plus 8 for the shamash.

Where to place it?

  • In a doorway: following the Chabad-Lubavitch custom, it is placed in the frame of an inside doorway, on the side opposite the mezuzah, so that when we walk through we are surrounded by both mitzvot.
  • In a window: it can also be placed on the sill of a window facing the street to publicise the miracle (as long as it is no more than about 9 metres above street level).

Safety tip

Always place it on a stable, fireproof surface, away from curtains and out of reach of small children.

Lighting, step by step

  1. Gather the whole family around the menorah.
  2. Light the shamash first and hold it in your right hand (or your left if you are left-handed).
  3. Standing, say the blessings for that night.
  4. Light the night’s candles from left to right, always starting with the new candle added that day. Candles are placed in the menorah from right to left as the days go by, but they are lit from left to right.
  5. Put the shamash back in its holder.
  6. Say “Hanerot Halalu” and “Mizmor Shir Chanukat Habayit”, and sing the hymn “Ma’oz Tzur”.
  7. Stay near the candles for at least half an hour (except on Friday afternoon). Remember that the light of the Chanukah candles may not be used for reading or working; we only look at them to give thanks and celebrate the miracle.

When to light it?

The Chabad-Lubavitch custom is to light the menorah shortly after sunset (or at nightfall), making sure it has enough fuel or large enough candles to burn until 30 minutes after nightfall (when the stars come out). Standard Chanukah candles last about 30 minutes, so they should be lit once it is already dark.

Mind Shabbat! · Friday 4 and Friday 11 December

Lighting a fire on Shabbat is forbidden. So on both the first night (Friday 4) and the eighth night (Friday 11), the menorah must be lit before the Shabbat candles (before 17:03 in Barcelona). Be sure to use large candles or enough oil to keep them burning at least until 18:30 (30 minutes after nightfall).

Saturday night · Saturday 5 December

The second candle is lit only after Shabbat has ended (from 18:10 in Barcelona) and after the Havdalah prayer has been said.

Getting home late? If you couldn’t light at the usual time, you can still light later in the night, as long as someone else at home is awake to see the lights.
