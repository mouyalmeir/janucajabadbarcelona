The Festival of Lights

What Chanukah is, where it comes from and why we light one more candle every night.

What is Chanukah?

Chanukah (also spelled Hanukkah) is the “Festival of Lights” of the Jewish calendar. It lasts eight days and is celebrated in winter by lighting the menorah (a nine-branched candelabrum) each night, reciting special blessings and enjoying traditional foods fried in oil.

The Hebrew word Chanukah means “inauguration” or “dedication”, and it commemorates the rededication of the Holy Temple in Jerusalem more than 2,100 years ago, after the victory of the Maccabees.

The story

In the 2nd century BCE, the Land of Israel came under the rule of the Syrian-Greek empire of Antiochus Epiphanes, who tried to force Hellenistic culture and rituals on the Jewish people: he banned the study of Torah and the observance of the commandments (mitzvot), and desecrated the Holy Temple in Jerusalem.

Against all odds, a small group of faithful but poorly armed Jews, led by Judah the Maccabee, stood up to one of the most powerful armies of the time.

  1. 2nd century BCEAntiochus Epiphanes bans the study of Torah and the mitzvot, and desecrates the Temple in Jerusalem.
  2. The revoltJudah the Maccabee and a handful of faithful Jews rise up against one of the most powerful armies of their time.
  3. 25 KislevThe Maccabees reclaim the Holy Temple and rededicate it.
  4. Eight days of lightA small jar of pure oil, enough for one day, burns for eight.
  5. TodayWe light the menorah every night to publicise the miracle: the mitzvah of Pirsumei Nisa.

Did you know…?

According to one tradition, the name Maccabee is an acronym of מִי כָמֹכָה בָּאֵלִם ה׳ (Mi Chamocha Ba’elim Hashem, “Who is like You among the mighty, O G-d?”), the motto they carried on their banners.

The two miracles

1

The victory

The few and weak Maccabees defeated the mighty Greek army, drove it out of the Land of Israel and reclaimed the Holy Temple on 25 Kislev.

2

The oil

When they went to light the Temple Menorah (the seven-branched candelabrum), they found that the Greeks had defiled almost all of the ritual olive oil. They found just one small jar still intact, bearing the seal of the High Priest, with enough oil to burn for a single day, while preparing new pure oil would take eight days. Miraculously, that small amount of oil burned for eight full days and nights.

To commemorate and publicise these miracles, the Sages established the festival of Chanukah.

A little light dispels a lot of darkness

The Maccabees fought spiritual darkness with courage; today we do it by adding light. Darkness is simply the absence of light, so every good deed, however small it may seem, has an immense impact.
